01 · The thesis

The rules of marketing
are changing. Fast.

The next great marketing teams won't look like agencies. They'll operate like boutique hedge funds: everything they run gets a price, and money moves to what works. Daily.

India isn’t a market.

02 · The machine

This is APERTURE.

An aperture controls exposure: how much gets through, and where. One loop: money goes out, four kinds of receipts come back, and one table decides what happens the next day.

Click any block to trace its path through the money · on a desktop, hover for a one-line description

humans decide machine moves money audit gate platform-run, their AI evidence coming back winners re-run as paid, from the creator’s own handle

What “moves ₹” means. The brain reads the ledger and puts more behind what is working: run the line again, extend the format, take it to the next audience. What is not working stops taking spend, and that budget returns to the pool. Either way, the money follows the evidence.

OpenTesting the allocator · what is the rule actually worth?

Two budgeting rules, one synthetic market. Twenty-four campaigns carry true costs the allocator cannot see, and every one of them fatigues over ninety days. Both lines spend the same monthly budget. The grey line splits it evenly and holds; the coloured line re-prices each campaign from what came back and moves the money. The gap between them is what the rule is worth.

What the model assumes. No funnel is modelled and no conversion rate is assumed: each campaign is simply given a hidden true cost per install, and installs are spend divided by that cost. Everything is indexed to the equal split at 100, because the size of the lead does not depend on the cost level. In a separate run, scaling every campaign four times cheaper and then four times dearer left the lead at +51%, which is why the chart is drawn as an index rather than in rupees. There is no control for that on this page.

And the allocator’s four most recent moves, in its own words:

A slow start is not a verdict. Money concentrates from day one, so a line can lose share in its first week. Two things bring it back. Every campaign keeps a small share of the budget for all ninety days, so nothing is ever switched off. And old evidence fades daily, so a bad first week stops counting once a line improves. In one separate run, a line planted nine times too expensive and repaired on day 55 was the biggest position by day 90. Recovery took five weeks, so a late repair runs out of runway.

The edge depends on spread. Make every campaign cost nearly the same and moving money is worth almost nothing. Widen the gap and the rule is worth more. So the number to look at is the real book above: 110x apart in views inside one follower tier, against a fee range of about 3x. Same settings always give the same market.

03 · The case study

Wispr for lawyers
and law students.

Less time drafting. More time thinking.

Two million advocates in India already dictate for a living. Their judgment is what a client is paying for, and the day around it fills up with drafting, formatting, note-taking and logging time. Hand those hours back and they go to the work only a lawyer can do.

2,013,081 advocates are enrolled with the state bar councils (Ministry of Law and Justice, figures reported to the Rajya Sabha). It is the one number on this page that is measured rather than modelled.

What is already built

Wispr has already shipped
the product for lawyers.

wisprflow.ai/lawyers is live, and almost nobody in India has seen it. Four things on it carry the hours around the argument:

DRAFTINGSpeak the analysis, get the document. Dictation with AI edits turns spoken argument into formatted text inside the app the lawyer is already in, so contracts, briefs and case notes come out review-ready.
VOCABULARYA personal dictionary and a snippet library. Flow learns the terms a lawyer actually uses, and voice shortcuts fire whole clauses: confidentiality, billing terms, privilege disclaimers.
MEETINGSNotetaker, and it is new. Client consultations and chamber briefings become notes accurate enough to act on, across every meeting platform. This is the piece nobody in India has been shown.
BILLINGThe meeting becomes the claim. Billable entries get logged by voice as the work happens, which is the single most-skipped task in the profession. For someone who bills by time, a recovered hour is a billed hour.

What it needs for India: the dictionary ships seeded with Sarbanes-Oxley, SCOTUS, Obergefell and Weil Gotshal, and the snippet library with American NDAs and privacy policies. Swap in the BNS and BNSS, India’s new criminal codes, plus Indian case names and court formats, and the same product lands here. That is a vocabulary change rather than a rebuild.

The wedge

Give every law student
a year of Flow. Free.

REACHLegal-education creators and campus champions carry it. Each one is priced the way a reel is priced, ranked on what similar audiences have already cost and checked for disclosure before it ships.
THE OFFERA full year for the national law universities, claimed through a link of its own so every campus is one priced row. A free seat costs almost nothing to serve, and buying the same cohort with ads costs real money.
TIMINGLaw has a delivery schedule built in. Internships land in May and in November, so the habit walks from campus into chambers twice a year. The intern drafts by voice. The senior notices.
PAYMENTThe money comes last. A year of dictation builds a personal dictionary of case names and court formats that nobody wants to rebuild elsewhere. Graduates convert at half price, and firms sign per-seat annuals once five people on one domain are active.
OpenThe order of attack, and the data flywheel

The order of attack. Top firms first, roughly 500 major firms and an estimated 30,000 fee-earners: what a top-firm associate uses, every junior copies. Then metros, then tier-2 courts and firms, where Hindi and Hinglish are the working language and the product is strongest. Districts follow the price curve down.

The data flywheel, consent-first. Every opted-in lawyer teaches Flow the language of Indian law: section citations, Latin maxims, case-name pronunciation, drafting conventions. Vocabulary, never privileged content. By year two that is the largest corpus of Indian legal dictation anywhere, and it becomes Flow Legal, a model no competitor can match without these same users. Upside only; nothing on this page depends on it.

B2B · law firms

How a firm gets sold.

One line, left to right. The firm arrives on the list because its own people put it there.

TARGETThe top tier first: Cyril Amarchand, Shardul Amarchand, AZB, Khaitan, Trilegal, JSA and Luthra. Cyril Amarchand went AI-first in January 2025 and put Harvey and Lucio in front of its lawyers, so the intent and the budget are already there. Then in-house legal at the NIFTY-100, and the legal-process outsourcers. The buyer is the managing partner or the general counsel; the champion is the associate already using Flow.
OUTREACHThe intro comes from a person, on WhatsApp or in chambers, because that is where the bar already talks. Two things move a partner: which peer firms use it, and what happens to privileged content, so the one-page case carries both with zero data retention stated plainly. Pricing is a rupee per-seat annual after a pilot.
PROOFOn day 30 the managing partner gets his own firm’s usage report next to the quote. Words dictated, hours recovered, who is using it. The firm closes itself on its own numbers, in the same ledger as every creator line.
